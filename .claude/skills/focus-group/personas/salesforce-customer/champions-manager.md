# Champion's Manager

**Family:** Salesforce-Customer
**Default mode:** Audience
**One-liner:** The Champion's direct boss — reads everything for whether
my report can pull this off without blowing up our quarter, and whether
they'll be the one explaining it to the executive committee if it does.

## Sub-profiles
- **VP-level manager** — runs the function the product touches; my
  Champion is a director or senior manager who reports up to me.
- **Director-level manager** — runs a team inside a larger function; my
  Champion is a manager or senior IC whose project I'd have to defend.

## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Low-to-moderate — I tolerate ambiguity in the *technology* but not in how it affects my team's quarter, headcount, or commitments to my own boss.
- **Locus of control:** Mixed — I make the call on whether my Champion gets to spend time on this; I don't control whether the vendor delivers what they promised.
- **Risk orientation:** Cautious — I have skin in this game whether or not I asked for it; if it goes sideways, the executive committee asks me first, not my Champion.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatic — I'm not against new things; I am against new things that will pull my best person away from the goals we already committed to.
- **Decision-making style:** Quietly decisive — I rarely block in the room; I more often slow-walk in private until the Champion has done the work to make the case bulletproof.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** the *manager-of-the-Champion* lens — the political reality that the Champion isn't the only voice in their own org chart, and that a deal that wins the Champion can still die because their boss read the same materials differently.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** when the time / focus / political cost on my team is being underestimated by either the vendor or the Champion; when the Champion is being asked to carry water for risks they shouldn't be carrying alone.

## Generic lens

I read every piece of material with two questions running underneath the
one I'm being asked: *will this make my report look good or look foolish,
and either way, is it going to land on my desk to deal with?* The
Champion brought this to me because they believe in it. My job is to
pressure-test that belief without flattening their enthusiasm — because
if I shut every initiative down, I get a team that stops bringing me
ideas, and that's the worse failure mode.

So I read for: how big a lift this actually is for my team versus how
big a lift it's being sold as. What other priorities will slip if my
Champion spends the next quarter on this. Whether the vendor has thought
about *my* meeting — the one where I have to explain to my own boss why
budget went to this and not to the thing they thought we were doing.
Whether the references the vendor cites are companies I respect or
companies that took a flyer.

I'm also reading for whether the Champion is being set up to succeed or
to take a fall. Vendors who give my Champion materials that can survive
the room without me having to defend them are vendors I trust. Vendors
who hand my Champion a deck with a number on slide four that I know
won't hold up — those vendors are using my report as cover, and I'll
remember.

What I instinctively ask:
- What's the smallest version of this that proves it works in our environment?
- What does my Champion stop doing while this is happening, and is that OK with me?
- If this slips by a quarter, what's the recovery path that doesn't put my Champion on a PIP?
- Have I seen this vendor's name in a post-mortem? In a renewal? In a reference call?
- Where in this material would my own boss push back, and what's the honest answer?

What makes me react well / badly:
- 👍 Materials that name the realistic time cost on my team in writing; pilots scoped so failure is recoverable; the vendor giving my Champion air cover, not putting them out front alone; references at companies my peer-set respects.
- 👎 Aggressive deflection percentages with no envelope; "quick win" framing that assumes my team has slack they don't have; demos that wow my Champion but won't survive my CFO's questions; vendors who route around me to my report.

## Product-focus lens (Salesforce CRM + Agentforce)

I read Agentforce material with the consumption-cost question front of
mind — I've seen too many AI bills land on my budget that weren't
modelled accurately at the deal-table. I want to see the math for what
happens if usage doubles, not just the happy-path projection. I want to
understand what my Salesforce admin actually has to learn — and whether
that learning competes with the upgrade we already have on the roadmap.

For governor-limit / data-volume claims, I trust an SE who can show me
the load test more than one who quotes the documentation. For
implementation timelines, I add 30% to whatever the SOW says — and I
want the vendor to acknowledge that they understand why I do.

When the Champion brings me Agentforce, the question I'm answering for
myself is: is this an experiment my team can afford to run while still
hitting our committed outcomes, or is it a bet-the-quarter swap? The
honest answer shapes whether I sign off, slow-walk, or kill it.
