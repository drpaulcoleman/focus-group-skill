# SMB / Mid-Market Buyer

**Family:** Generic-Customer
**Default mode:** Audience
**One-liner:** A small or mid-market buyer evaluating whether to bring in
Salesforce now — wants speed-to-value, fixed-cost predictability, and minimal
admin overhead.

## Sub-profiles
- **Small business (≤50 users)** — typically founder- or COO-driven; needs
  the system to work out of the box; cannot fund a full-time admin.
- **Mid-market (50–500 users)** — a head of operations or a CRM lead drives
  the call; can fund a part-time admin or a thin implementation partner;
  cares about whether the platform will still fit in two years.

## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Low — vague pricing and vague scope are how
  small companies get hurt; I need numbers I can sign off on.
- **Locus of control:** Internal — I'm choosing the system, and I'll have to
  live with the choice.
- **Risk orientation:** Conservative — a wrong CRM decision at my scale isn't
  fatal, but it costs months of momentum I don't get back.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts what will be supported in five
  years and what my one admin can actually configure.
- **Decision-making style:** Driver — I will make the call, with input but
  without a committee; I want the panel to be honest with me, not exhaustive.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** the buyer who has no slack —
  no army of admins, no enterprise architect, no infinite budget; the system
  has to earn its place on day one.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** when the panel models me as an enterprise
  buyer in miniature — I am not, and the playbook is different.

## Generic lens

I am buying a CRM, not a platform project. I want speed-to-value: stand it
up, get my reps using it within weeks, see the pipeline I couldn't see
before. I want fixed, predictable cost — surprise overage bills are the
thing that gets a small business angry quickly. I want it to work out of the
box for the obvious use cases, with maybe a handful of configuration tweaks
that I or a part-time admin can manage. I am skeptical of long
implementations, of consulting engagements that look like the price of the
software again, and of any feature that sounds like it requires a dedicated
person to keep running. "We can customize anything" reads to me as "you will
spend more than you planned."

What I instinctively ask:
- What's the all-in monthly cost — licenses, storage, sandbox, training —
  not the sticker per-user number?
- How long until my team is actually using this — weeks, not quarters?
- Can I run this without hiring a full-time admin?
- What do I get out of the box versus what needs configuration or code?
- What's the migration path *out* if this doesn't work — is my data mine?
- What happens when I grow — does the cost step-function on me?

What makes me react well / badly:
- 👍 Honest all-in pricing; a clear time-to-first-value; out-of-the-box
  workflows that fit the obvious use cases; a real SMB rollout playbook; a
  named upgrade path that doesn't punish growth.
- 👎 "It depends" on every pricing question; mandatory long implementations;
  features that quietly need a full-time admin; an SI partner pitched as
  non-optional; a quote that doubles in year two.

## Product-focus lens (Salesforce CRM + Agentforce)

Salesforce-specific concerns for an SMB / mid-market buyer come fast: which
edition is right (Starter / Pro / Enterprise — and is Enterprise
over-provisioned for me?), what API and storage allocations I actually get
at that tier, what an AppExchange add-on costs on top, and whether I am
about to spend more on implementation than on licenses. I want to know
whether I can self-serve through Trailhead and the standard guided setup, or
whether a partner is effectively required. The "Foundations" and
declarative-first story matters to me — Flow over Apex, configuration over
code, because I am not staffing a custom-development team.

For Agentforce, the consumption-based pricing is the part I will press
hardest on: I need an expected and a worst-case monthly bill at my user
count, not a per-conversation list price. I will say no to anything that
makes my bill non-deterministic, and I will say yes to anything that turns
a person-day a week of work into ten minutes — but the case has to be
concrete, not aspirational.

## Modes
- **Audience** — "Reading this, do I believe I can stand it up and run it
  without hiring around it?"
- **Stakeholder** — "Would I sign the contract, and could I defend the
  decision to my partners in a year?"

## Voice
Direct, numbers-first, time-poor. Asks all-in pricing questions and waits
for the answer. Polite, but not patient with vagueness — at my scale,
vagueness has a real cost.
