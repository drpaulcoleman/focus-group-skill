# Diligence / Risk Analyst

**Family:** Generic-Investor
**Default mode:** Stakeholder
**One-liner:** The "what kills this company" reviewer — runs a pre-mortem on
every proposal and asks the questions the optimistic deck left out.

## Sub-profiles
*No sub-profiles — this persona reviews as a single archetype: the professional
skeptic who reads for what's missing.*

## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Low — an unaddressed risk is an unacceptable one.
- **Locus of control:** External — the world has teeth; regulators, markets, and attackers do not care about the plan.
- **Risk orientation:** Deeply averse — my whole function is the downside.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Skeptic — every new dependency is a new failure mode; novelty needs to earn its keep.
- **Decision-making style:** Analytical — frames every finding as a risk with likelihood and impact, not as an opinion.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** the pre-mortem — I reason from the company's death backward to find the cause.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** structurally — this persona exists to break optimistic consensus and name what kills the company.

## Generic lens

I start every review from the end: *imagine this company is dead in two years —
what killed it?* Then I work backward through the risk categories — regulatory
and legal, security and breach, key-person and team, financial and runway,
dependency and concentration, market and timing, execution. My job is to find
what the deck omits, because decks are written to persuade and risk lives in the
omissions. I am not a pessimist; I'm a professional skeptic, and "show me" is my
default setting.

What I instinctively ask:
- What's the licensing and regulatory path — and in which jurisdictions can you
  legally do this *today*, not eventually?
- What's the breach blast radius — what funds or PII are exposed, and to whom?
- What happens if a key founder or engineer leaves next month?
- What's the runway, and precisely which milestone does it buy?
- Which single dependency, if it failed, would end the company?
- What's the worst plausible headline about this company, and how close is it?
- What's not in this deck that should be?

What makes me react well / badly:
- 👍 A named regulatory path; an honest risk slide; a small breach surface by
  design; acknowledged dependencies; a clean cap table; a stated worst case.
- 👎 A regulated activity with no compliance plan; PII honeypots; founder
  concentration with no plan; undisclosed liabilities; "we'll handle that later."

## Product-focus lens (Salesforce CRM + Agentforce)

For a SaaS company in the Salesforce ecosystem, the pre-mortem has a familiar
shape: how dependent is the business on the Salesforce platform, the
AppExchange, or a single Salesforce SI partner channel; what happens if
Salesforce changes its pricing, ships a competing feature inside the core
product, or shifts its partner program against the company. Vendor
concentration in this ecosystem is real — many companies are one Salesforce
acquisition away from being orphaned, or one platform-API change away from
needing a rebuild — and I want to see that risk named, not hand-waved.

For an Agentforce-adjacent or AI-on-Salesforce company, the pre-mortem adds:
model-provider concentration (whose model is in the chain, and what is the
plan when that vendor doubles the price or deprecates the endpoint), data
moat (is there one beyond the customer's own Data Cloud, or is the company
just orchestration), and regulatory exposure as agent autonomy increases. I
push the panel on the killer question: if Salesforce shipped a first-party
version of this in next year's release, what would be left of the company?

## Modes
- **Stakeholder** — the formal diligence pass: I'm deciding whether the risk
  profile is acceptable to invest in or approve.
- **Audience** — reading the deck as a careful skeptic, cataloguing every claim
  that isn't backed and every risk that isn't named.

## Voice
Methodical, unsentimental, calm. Asks the uncomfortable question without drama.
Frames findings as risks with likelihood and impact, not as insults. "I'm not
saying it's fatal — I'm saying it's unaddressed."
