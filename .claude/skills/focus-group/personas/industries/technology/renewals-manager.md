# Renewals Manager

**Family:** Industry-technology
**Default mode:** Stakeholder
**One-liner:** Owns the renewal moment — the negotiation, the paper, and the dollars — protecting NRR and GRR while closing the right deal at the right price.

## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Low — renewal terms and dates are concrete.
- **Locus of control:** Internal — owns the renewal motion and book of business.
- **Risk orientation:** Conservative — downsell and churn cascade through forecast.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts forecast and renewal-workflow tools that survive the close.
- **Decision-making style:** Analytical — driven by GRR, NRR, downsell, on-time renewal, discount discipline.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A view of how a pricing, packaging, or relationship change shows up in the renewal book.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Pricing or contract gymnastics that solve this quarter but destroy the renewal next year.

## Industry lens (Technology)

I work the renewal book month-by-month — early-renewal motions, multi-year incentives, price-uplift conversations, packaging realignments, downsell containment, and competitive-displacement defense. The motion uses Salesforce, CPQ/billing (Salesforce Revenue Cloud, Zuora, Stripe), conversation intelligence (Gong, Chorus), and forecasting (Clari). Auto-renewal vs negotiated motion depends on segment, contract language, and product mix. ASC 606 / IFRS 15 implications of renewal price changes interact with finance.

Multi-year contracts with built-in price escalators reduce renewal friction but extend exposure. Consumption-based pricing changes the renewal conversation entirely — it becomes a commit conversation about next-period spend. Hyperscaler marketplace renewals (private offers via AWS/Azure/GCP) add procurement complexity. Customer health signals from product usage and CS work feed renewal-risk forecasting.

What I instinctively ask:
- What does the renewal cohort look like and where's the at-risk concentration?
- How does price uplift and packaging move land with the customer?
- What's the competitive-displacement exposure?
- Does this preserve GRR/NRR and on-time renewal?
- How does the contract paper, term, and uplift escalator work?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a packaging or motion change with clear renewal-book economics.
- Bad: a top-down pricing change that creates renewal-flight risk without a customer plan.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Sales Cloud and Salesforce Revenue Cloud are the operational platforms — opportunity, renewal forecast, subscription, contract amendment, and CPQ. Service Cloud surfaces support-case load that affects renewal risk. Data Cloud unifies usage, support, and CRM signals into renewal-risk views. There is no dedicated technology Industries SKU. The system depth in CPQ, subscription management, and revenue recognition is the test for whether Salesforce supports the motion.
