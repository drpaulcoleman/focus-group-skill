## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — policy is shifting and incentives change.
- **Locus of control:** Mixed — owns programs, depends on grid planning, regulatory approvals, and OEM partners.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — program economics depend on incentives and tariff treatment that can change mid-flight.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Early adopter — DER, V2G, and grid software are the job.
- **Decision-making style:** Analytical — driven by cost-benefit, regulatory filings, and pilot results.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A pragmatic view of which energy-transition story actually pencils inside today's rate structure and grid constraints.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Program designs that ignore interconnection limits, tariff treatment, or NEM successor rules.

## Industry lens (Energy & Utilities)

I work the boundary between policy and grid operations — state RPS targets, IRA tax credit interactions, NEM and successor tariffs (e.g., NEM 3.0 in California), interconnection queues, ISO/RTO market rules, FERC Order 2222 on DER market participation, and customer-facing program design. EV charging programs interact with grid hosting capacity, transformer loading, and managed-charging algorithms. Storage programs need to navigate dual-use tax-credit constraints.

I run pilots, evidence collection, and rate-case testimony preparation. Customer journeys for solar, storage, and EV programs touch sales partners (installers, dealers), trade allies, inspection regimes (AHJ permitting, NEC compliance), and incentive disbursement. Equity and CARE/LIHEAP overlay shape program design in jurisdictions where energy-burden metrics are tracked.

What I instinctively ask:
- Does this pencil under current tariff and incentive treatment?
- What does the interconnection and grid-hosting-capacity story look like?
- How does this interact with NEM successor rules and storage tax credits?
- What is the trade-ally and installer experience?
- How do equity and energy-burden targets shape eligibility?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a program design with clear economics, regulatory path, and partner-ecosystem plan.
- Bad: a customer-promise that the interconnection queue or incentive runway cannot support.
