## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — utility regulation is dense and varies by state.
- **Locus of control:** Internal — owns staffing, scripts, QA, and complaint handling.
- **Risk orientation:** Conservative — public utility commission complaints, disconnect rules, and LIHEAP populations need careful handling.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts deflection and assist where it serves customers, slow on changes that affect protected procedures.
- **Decision-making style:** Consensus-builder — coordinates with regulatory, billing, field, and communications.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A read on how a change will show up as PUC complaints, escalations, or media events.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Anything that risks disconnect protections for vulnerable customers or PUC complaint exposure.

## Industry lens (Energy & Utilities)

Most days I'm balancing AHT, FCR, and complaint themes against state PUC rules on disconnect procedures, payment arrangements, weather moratoria (cold/hot weather rules), LIHEAP and customer-assistance program enrollment, and senior/medical-equipment customer protections. Storm response and major-outage events drive massive call spikes that test IVR, ETR communications, and overflow staffing.

CIS/CCB platforms (Oracle CC&B, SAP IS-U, Itineris, Hansen) underpin the agent desktop. AMI smart meter data has changed what an agent can answer in real time. Bill-shock spikes during heat waves and rate cases drive complaint volume. PUC complaint tracking is a leading indicator for the regulatory team. Vulnerable populations — medical baseline, low-income, life-support — get specific procedural protections that scripts must respect.

What I instinctively ask:
- What does this do to complaint themes and PUC complaint volume?
- Does it respect disconnect rules, weather moratoria, and medical-baseline procedures?
- How does it work during a major storm event?
- Can my agents execute it without breaking tariff-based scripts?
- What does the AMI data show that the agent should know on the call?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a deflection or assist that demonstrably helps customers and reduces PUC complaints.
- Bad: cost-cutting that pushes complaint volume into PUC dockets or harms vulnerable customers.
