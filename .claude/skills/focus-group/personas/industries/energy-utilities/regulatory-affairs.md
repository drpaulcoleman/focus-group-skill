- **Locus of control:** Mixed — owns regulatory strategy and filings, depends on operations, finance, and customer programs.
- **Risk orientation:** Conservative — bad filings or missed reporting trigger enforcement and reputational damage.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Late majority — regulatory cycles outlast software cycles.

- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A view of how every program, rate, and disclosure will read in the public record and on the witness stand.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Anything that risks tariff violation, FERC standards of conduct, or NERC CIP/reliability standard non-compliance.

## Industry lens (Energy & Utilities)

I work rate cases, tariff filings, compliance reports (FERC Form 1, EIA forms, PUC quarterly reports), integrated resource plans, performance-based ratemaking proceedings, and intervenor management. NERC reliability standards (CIP for cyber, others for operations) apply to bulk-power participants. EPA rules (mercury, GHG, water-quality) touch generation portfolios. State commissions vary widely in their philosophies, intervenor activity, and consumer-advocate posture.

Customer-facing programs (DER, EV, demand response, energy efficiency) require regulatory approval and cost-recovery structures. Wildfire-mitigation plans in fire-prone states create a parallel regulatory regime. Privacy-of-usage data, third-party access, and AMI program prudency reviews come up regularly. The regulatory record is permanent and public — every commitment becomes part of the next case.

What I instinctively ask:
- Is this consistent with our last rate case and our compliance plan?
- What does the tariff say, and do we need a filing?
- How will intervenors and consumer advocates read this?
- What's the cost-recovery mechanism and the prudency story?
- Does this touch NERC CIP, FERC standards of conduct, or EPA obligations?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a program with a clear regulatory path, cost-recovery story, and intervenor strategy.
- Bad: an operational change that creates regulatory exposure without a coordinated filing plan.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Salesforce is rarely the system of record for regulatory work, but Energy & Utilities Cloud's program data (DER enrollments, demand-response participation, EV program eligibility) feeds compliance reports and rate-case evidence. Data Cloud matters as the unified-customer signal layer. Service Cloud handles regulatory complaint workflows where they land. I read every program design through whether the resulting data set will survive prudency review and discovery.
