## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Low — order routing has to be deterministic.
- **Locus of control:** Internal — owns the OMS, routing rules, and exception management.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — split shipments, missed promises, and return fraud erode margin and trust.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts AI in routing and fraud detection when it survives stores' operational reality.
- **Decision-making style:** Analytical — driven by ship-cost, on-time, store-fulfillment success rate, return rate.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A view of whether a customer promise survives inventory accuracy and store-fulfillment reality.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Customer promises that the OMS, inventory accuracy, or store capacity can't keep.

## Industry lens (Retail)

I work in OMS platforms (Manhattan Active Omni, Salesforce Order Management, Fluent Commerce, IBM Sterling, in-house) and inventory-visibility systems. KPIs are perfect-order rate, ship-cost per order, store-fulfillment success rate, click-to-deliver time, BOPIS pick rate, return rate, and reverse-logistics cost. Inventory accuracy (RFID, cycle count, store-level audit) is foundational — without it, every promise is at risk.

Carrier diversification, last-mile experiments (Instacart, DoorDash partnerships, micro-fulfillment), and same-day capability rewrite economics. Return mechanics (in-store, by-mail, drop-off partner) are increasingly central — Amazon-style frictionless returns have become customer expectation, and return fraud is real. Payments orchestration (BNPL, digital wallets, gift cards, store credit) intersects with order management. Cross-border ecommerce adds compliance (HTS classification, duties, country-of-origin) overlays.

What I instinctively ask:
- Does inventory accuracy support this promise?
- Can stores actually fulfill at the assumed success rate during peak?
- What does this do to ship cost and split-shipment rate?
- How does the return flow and fraud-control story hold?
- Are payments and promotions consistent across channels?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a customer promise backed by realistic inventory and store-fulfillment capacity.
- Bad: a marketing claim of "available everywhere" that the OMS and inventory can't actually deliver.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Commerce Cloud B2C and Salesforce Order Management are the direct platforms — order capture, inventory visibility, sourcing/routing, store-fulfillment workflows, return management, and customer service order context. Service Cloud handles order-related cases. Data Cloud unifies order, customer, and inventory signals into the omnichannel view. Marketing Cloud handles transactional comms. There is no dedicated retail Industries SKU; OMS + core clouds is the package.
