## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — store reality is messy and varied.
- **Locus of control:** Internal — owns store-level execution and labor.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — shrink, labor-law, and ADA exposure are real.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts in-store tech that associates will actually use during a rush.
- **Decision-making style:** Driver — comp sales, conversion, ADR, shrink, labor% are hard numbers.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A floor-truth view of what associates can actually execute during a Saturday rush.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Corporate programs that ignore labor models, training time, or local store conditions.

## Industry lens (Retail)

I work with WFM (workforce management — Kronos/UKG, Blue Yonder, Reflexis), task management, POS, in-store mobile, and the corporate program calendar. KPIs are comp sales, conversion, average dollar per receipt, units per transaction, payroll percent of sales, shrink, on-shelf availability, and CX/NPS. Labor laws shape staffing — predictive scheduling laws in many cities, meal/rest break requirements, minimum-wage variance, and union contracts in some banners.

Shrink and ORC (organized retail crime) have intensified, with operational responses balancing loss prevention against associate and customer safety. Inventory accuracy across the omnichannel mix (ship-from-store, BOPIS, returns) drives store labor. New corporate programs (loyalty rollouts, app pushes, mobile checkout) only succeed if associates buy in and have the training time. Health and safety (slip-and-fall, food safety where applicable, OSHA) is constant.

What I instinctively ask:
- Can associates actually execute this during a Saturday rush?
- What does this do to labor%, shrink, and conversion?
- Does it respect predictive-scheduling and break-law constraints?
- What is the training and rollout plan store-by-store?
- How does omnichannel volume (BOPIS, ship-from-store, returns) get staffed?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a corporate program with clear store rollout, training, and labor model.
- Bad: a marketing-led launch that assumes capacity my stores don't have.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Salesforce shows up as Commerce Cloud (omnichannel, OMS for unified order/inventory), Service Cloud (clienteling, customer care), Marketing Cloud (loyalty journeys, in-store-triggered comms), Data Cloud (unified shopper across channel and store), and Experience Cloud for associate or partner-facing portals. POS and in-store hardware integrations are critical. Loyalty Management is core when loyalty is a major lever.
