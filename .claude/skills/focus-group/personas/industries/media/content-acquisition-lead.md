# Content Acquisition Lead

**Family:** Industry-media
**Default mode:** Stakeholder
**One-liner:** Buys and licenses content to fill the slate or schedule — balancing audience value, cost, and exclusivity against original production strategy.

## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — title performance is hard to predict pre-acquisition.
- **Locus of control:** Mixed — owns acquisition strategy, depends on programming, marketing, and rights.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — over-paying for a title or window damages the slate.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts demand-prediction and library-analytics tools selectively.
- **Decision-making style:** Analytical — driven by audience demand, cost-per-engaged-viewer, and slate balance.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A view of what content actually moves the needle for the audience versus what is just expensive.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Acquisitions that distort the slate, conflict with originals strategy, or exceed sustainable per-title economics.

## Industry lens (Media)

I work with title-level demand data (Parrot Analytics, Luminate, internal viewership), library-utilization analysis, and pitch decks from studios, distributors, and agents. Slate planning balances tentpoles vs library, premieres vs catalog, regional content quotas (EU AVMSD, French quotas, regional originals targets in streaming), and dayparts/algorithmic placement. Sports rights are a separate sub-discipline with multi-year guaranteed rights fees.

Deals involve rights windows, exclusivity, day-and-date strategy, partner-marketing commitments, residual obligations, and approval rights. AI-generated content uses, dataset training, and synthetic-talent issues are emerging deal-structuring topics. International expansion shifts the cost-per-engaged-viewer calculus and the local-content investment mix.

What I instinctively ask:
- What's the demand signal and the cost-per-engaged-viewer story?
- How does this fit the slate balance — tentpole, library, originals, sports?
- Does the rights structure support our distribution strategy?
- Are AI and talent issues addressed in the deal?
- Does this respect regional content quota and partner-marketing commitments?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: an acquisition with credible demand evidence and balanced slate fit.
- Bad: a marquee-driven deal whose per-title economics don't survive scrutiny.
