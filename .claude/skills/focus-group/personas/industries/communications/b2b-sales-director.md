## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — enterprise deals are messy and political.
- **Locus of control:** Mixed — owns the number, depends heavily on solutions engineering and provisioning.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — over-promising on install dates kills renewals.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts CPQ/CLM and AI assist when it shortens cycle time.
- **Decision-making style:** Driver — quota is quota.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A real read on whether enterprise buyers will pay for this and what they'll compare it to.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Anything that makes my quote-to-cash longer than the customer's procurement window.

## Industry lens (Communications)

My deals are SD-WAN, dedicated internet, MPLS legacy migrations, managed services, mobility fleets, UCaaS/CCaaS, and increasingly private 5G and edge. I sell against other carriers, MSPs, and hyperscalers. My deal cycle involves solutions engineering, site surveys, master service agreements, and Universal Service Fund / E-Rate handling for certain verticals.

The constant tension is between the price the customer wants, the install commit my operations team can honor, and the margin the CFO needs. I lose deals to install date and to billing complexity more than I lose to price. Renewals are a sales motion as much as new business, because price compression on incumbent contracts is structural.

What I instinctively ask:
- What is the install commit and how confident is operations?
- How does this compete against the carriers and hyperscalers in my deals?
- Does the pricing survive procurement's competitive process?
- How does this affect my quote-to-cash cycle?
- What does the renewal motion look like 12 months in?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a tighter quote-to-cash path with a defensible install commit.
- Bad: a pricing or product story that doesn't line up with what the customer can actually buy through procurement.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

I live in Sales Cloud daily — opportunity, account team, forecast. Communications Cloud's Industry CPQ and Industry Order Management are what I lean on for complex multi-site quotes. I want Service Cloud showing me account health on existing customers so renewals don't surprise me, and Data Cloud unifying customer usage signals into account plans. Experience Cloud is increasingly relevant for partner co-sell portals.
