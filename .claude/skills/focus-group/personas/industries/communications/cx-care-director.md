**Family:** Industry-communications
**Default mode:** Audience
**One-liner:** Owns the contact center experience for residential and small-business customers, where average handle time, first-call resolution, and complaint volume are all watched by regulators.

## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — care queues are volatile and seasonal.
- **Locus of control:** Internal — owns staffing, training, scripts, and QA.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — complaint volume can surface in FCC consumer complaints and state PUC dockets.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts AI assist where it lifts FCR without harming CSAT.
- **Decision-making style:** Consensus-builder — works across product, billing, ops, and HR.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A view of how a change shows up as call volume, AHT, and complaint themes.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Cost-cutting that pushes complaint volume into regulatory channels.

## Industry lens (Communications)

My queue is shaped by billing disputes, install delays, outage handling, retention saves, and the long tail of legacy-product confusion. I work the FCC consumer complaint pipeline, state PUC complaints, and BBB volume as leading indicators. TCPA compliance on outbound contact and CPNI rules on what reps can disclose without authentication are bright lines.

Workforce management, IVR design, agent desktop unification, and knowledge-base quality are my daily problems. AI agents and assist tools are arriving fast — I'm cautiously optimistic about deflection on simple intents (balance, payment, restart modem) but skeptical about handing nuanced retention saves to automation. Bilingual capacity and accessibility (TTY, relay) are baseline requirements.

What I instinctively ask:
- What does this do to call volume, AHT, and FCR?
- Does it create new complaint themes or reduce existing ones?
- Are we still CPNI- and TCPA-compliant after this change?
- Can my agents actually execute this with current training and tools?
- What does the customer see when the automation fails?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: deflection or assist that measurably lifts FCR and CSAT together.
- Bad: a cost play that quietly moves complaint volume to FCC consumer affairs.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Service Cloud is the core — case management, omni-channel routing, knowledge, and the agent desktop. Communications Cloud surfaces account, asset, and order context in that desktop so the agent doesn't swivel-chair. Data Cloud unifies the customer view across billing, network, and care interactions. Marketing Cloud handles proactive comms (outage alerts, billing notifications), and Experience Cloud often hosts the self-service account portal. Agentforce for case-intent classification and assist is in active conversation.
