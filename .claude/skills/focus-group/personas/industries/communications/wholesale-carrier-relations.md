## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — contracts are dense, settlements are slow.
- **Locus of control:** Mixed — owns commercial terms, depends on operations and regulatory.
- **Risk orientation:** Conservative — bad interconnect deals leak revenue for years.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Late majority — wholesale moves slowly and contracts last.
- **Decision-making style:** Analytical — settlement math, dispute trails, contract clause-by-clause.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A reminder that the business has counterparties, not just customers, and that those counterparties have leverage.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Anything that breaks an existing interconnect or settlement structure without a clear migration and dispute plan.

## Industry lens (Communications)

My world is interconnection agreements, settlement systems, STIR/SHAKEN attestation flows, robocall mitigation reporting, traffic pumping disputes, and number-resource management (NANP). FCC and state PUC rules govern much of what I do, and TRACED Act enforcement has reshaped voice termination economics in the US. Internationally I deal with ITU frameworks, sanctions screening on destinations, and fraud (Wangiri, IRSF) that can lose millions in a weekend.

I am also the operator of MVNO host relationships, wholesale fiber leases (IRUs), and the partner side of any "network-as-a-service" or 5G-slicing wholesale story. New product launches need a wholesale view because access to the customer often runs through a counterparty's network.

What I instinctively ask:
- How does this affect settlement and reciprocal compensation?
- Are we STIR/SHAKEN-compliant and robocall-mitigation-current on this traffic?
- What's the fraud exposure profile?
- Does this touch existing wholesale or interconnect contracts and how?
- What is the dispute-handling and chargeback path?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a wholesale story with clear settlement math and a fraud-control plan.
- Bad: a retail product launch that assumes wholesale access is free and instantaneous.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens
