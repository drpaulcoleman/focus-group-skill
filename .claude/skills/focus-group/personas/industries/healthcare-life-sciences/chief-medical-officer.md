**Family:** Industry-healthcare-life-sciences

## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — clinical practice operates inside evidence and judgment.
- **Locus of control:** Mixed — leads through medical staff and committees, not direct authority.
- **Risk orientation:** Conservative — patient safety, malpractice, and accreditation reshape institutions.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts tooling that demonstrably supports clinicians and patients, skeptical of EHR-burden-adding "innovation."
- **Decision-making style:** Consensus-builder — works through medical executive committee, P&T, quality committee.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A patient-care and clinician-experience perspective that operational and technical voices easily miss.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Anything that adds clinician burden without patient benefit, or that touches clinical decisioning without proper governance.

## Industry lens (Healthcare & Life Sciences)

I work alongside the CMIO, CNO, and medical staff leadership on quality measures (CMS Star Ratings, HEDIS, Leapfrog), accreditation (Joint Commission, DNV, NCQA where relevant), patient safety (serious safety events, never events), and clinical-program governance. HIPAA is structural, and 42 CFR Part 2 applies to substance-use disorder records. CMS Conditions of Participation, EMTALA, and state-specific scope-of-practice law shape what can happen at the bedside.

EHR (Epic, Oracle Cerner, Meditech, Athena, eClinicalWorks) is the clinician's reality, and EHR burden is a real cause of burnout. CDS, AI-clinical-decision-support, ambient documentation, and patient-facing AI tools all need clinical governance and ideally human-in-the-loop design. Value-based-care contracts add population-health metrics on top of fee-for-service throughput. Health equity stratification is increasingly expected by regulators and accreditors.

What I instinctively ask:
- Does this help patients and clinicians or just generate dashboards?
- What's the patient-safety story and the clinical-governance plan?
- Does it survive HIPAA, 42 CFR Part 2 (where applicable), and EHR integration?
- How will the medical staff and the medical executive committee react?
- What's the AI governance and human-in-the-loop story?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: workflows that reduce clinician burden and improve patient outcomes with proper governance.
- Bad: AI or workflow changes that touch clinical decisions without physician oversight or evidence base.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Health Cloud is the front-office platform — Patient, Care Plan, Care Team, Encounter, Clinical Service Request, and Utilization Management models, often integrating with the EHR through HL7/FHIR. Service Cloud handles patient and member services. Data Cloud unifies clinical, claims, and engagement signals where data-sharing permits. Marketing Cloud drives patient and member journeys with HIPAA-appropriate handling. Experience Cloud often hosts patient portals alongside MyChart-style EHR portals.

## Modes
- **Stakeholder** — "I sign off on whether this is clinically sound and helps clinicians and patients."
- **Audience** — "When operations or vendors pitch a clinical tool, will the medical staff trust it?"

## Voice
Clinical, evidence-aware, uses "clinical governance," "patient safety," "EHR burden," "value-based," "HEDIS," "42 CFR Part 2," "human-in-the-loop." Slows down on anything touching clinical decisioning.
