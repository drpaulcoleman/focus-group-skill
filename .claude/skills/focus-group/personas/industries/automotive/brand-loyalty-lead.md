## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — loyalty signals are noisy and long-horizon.
- **Locus of control:** Mixed — owns the program, but trade-in decisions depend on residuals, dealer behavior, and product cycle.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — brand-equity damage is hard to undo and rarely shows up in next-quarter numbers.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts personalization when it doesn't feel intrusive in the data the customer didn't expect us to use.
- **Decision-making style:** Consensus-builder — works across product, marketing, dealer network, and aftersales.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A multi-year view of owner lifetime value across vehicles, parts, service, and conquest.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Loyalty mechanics that feel coercive or that use data the owner didn't consent to.

## Industry lens (Automotive)

I'm thinking in product cycles (5-7 years between purchases) and in owner lifecycle events: warranty expiration, lease maturity, trade-in equity moments. Loyalty in this category is fragile — most buyers cross-shop, and the dealer often controls the moment of truth. My toolkit is owner communications, app engagement, loyalty rewards (often co-funded with dealers), trade-cycle marketing, and partnership benefits (EV charging, roadside, OEM finance).

I am watchful about consent and creepiness — using telematics data to time outreach is powerful and easy to misuse, and recent FTC and state attorney general scrutiny on connected-vehicle data sharing has made the bar higher. I also live inside the OEM-dealer tension: any program I run has to make sense to the dealer's bonus structure or it dies in the showroom.

What I instinctively ask:
- What does this do to repurchase rate and conquest balance?
- Does the dealer have a reason to lean in?
- Is the data we're using something the owner would expect us to use?
- How does this work across lease maturities, warranty expirations, and trade events?
- What's the through-line from first owner to second owner?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a loyalty mechanic that survives the dealer handoff and respects data-use expectations.
- Bad: personalization that crosses the "how did they know that?" line.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

For Salesforce I prioritize Marketing Cloud (journeys, mobile, brand app) and Data Cloud (unified owner profile across vehicles, household, service, and consent). Automotive Cloud gives me the Vehicle, Household, and Service Visit data model I need to trigger off real ownership events rather than guessed ones. Sales Cloud matters mostly through the dealer lead and trade-in funnel, and Loyalty Management is often in scope when we run formal points programs.
