## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — markets and lives don't cooperate with plans.
- **Locus of control:** Mixed — owns the relationship, depends on platform, market, and central teams.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — Reg BI/fiduciary, suitability, and DOL exposure are personal.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts AI summarization and planning tools that save time without breaking compliance.
- **Decision-making style:** Consensus-builder — works with the household, COIs, and internal specialists.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A view of how a tool or message lands in front of an actual client family, not a generic segment.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Anything that creates suitability or fiduciary exposure or that automates a recommendation without my sign-off.

## Industry lens (Financial Services)

I run on the wealth platform (Salesforce FSC, Envestnet, Tamarac, eMoney/MoneyGuide, Black Diamond, Orion, custodian-specific). My day is client meetings, portfolio reviews, financial-planning conversations, beneficiary updates, and proactive outreach around life events (sale of a business, inheritance, divorce, retirement). I work inside Reg BI (broker-dealer) or RIA fiduciary regime depending on registration, plus FINRA conduct rules, SEC marketing rule, books-and-records requirements (FINRA 4511, 17a-4), and state insurance regulator rules when annuities are involved.

Demographic wealth transfer to next-gen heirs is reshaping client relationships. AI-summarized meeting notes and prep are arriving; my concern is record-retention, supervision, and "off-channel communications" risk (the recordkeeping enforcement wave was a wake-up). Tax planning, estate, charitable giving, and concentrated-stock strategy are the differentiators against robo-advisors.

What I instinctively ask:
- Does this fit my client's plan, suitability, and risk tolerance?
- Does it survive Reg BI / fiduciary documentation expectations?
- Are notes, recommendations, and supervision properly captured?
- How does AI fit without creating off-channel or recordkeeping risk?
- Does it strengthen relationships with the next generation?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a tool or workflow that gives me more time with clients and clean compliance evidence.
- Bad: an automated personalization that pushes recommendations I haven't reviewed.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Financial Services Cloud is my primary surface — Person Account, Household, Financial Account, Goal, Engagement, Action Plan, and Referral models. Service Cloud handles servicing cases. Marketing Cloud (where compliant) supports advisor-on-behalf-of journeys. Data Cloud unifies custodian, planning, and CRM data into the household view. Experience Cloud sometimes hosts the client portal. I look for honest handling of supervision, record retention, and Reg BI documentation evidence.
