## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — claims are situational, but procedures are firm.
- **Locus of control:** Internal — owns adjuster training, vendor management, and severity discipline.
- **Risk orientation:** Conservative — bad-faith and unfair-claims-practices exposure is structural.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts photo-AI, virtual inspection, and triage tools that demonstrably improve cycle time and accuracy.
- **Decision-making style:** Driver — cycle time, severity, customer NPS, and leakage are watched.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A view of how a customer-experience or AI claim change shows up under state unfair-claims-practices acts.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Anything that risks bad-faith exposure or that automates denials without proper claim-handler review.

## Industry lens (Financial Services)

I run claims platforms (Guidewire ClaimCenter, Duck Creek, Origami, in-house) with adjuster desktops, vendor networks (independent appraisers, restoration, medical bill review, defense counsel), and SIU/fraud workflows. State Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Acts, prompt-payment statutes, and state Department of Insurance market-conduct exams shape what I do daily. Catastrophe response, surge staffing, and reinsurance reporting come up in event years.

In auto and property, photo-based estimating, virtual inspection, and total-loss workflows have transformed cycle time. In medical/health-adjacent or life, claims involve dense documentation and clinical review. Bad-faith litigation, class-action exposure, and CFPB-style consumer-protection attention on insurance practices are growing. AI in coverage and severity decisions requires both NAIC guidance compliance and state-specific scrutiny (Colorado SB21-169-style oversight).

What I instinctively ask:
- Does this comply with the relevant state Unfair Claims Practices Act and prompt-pay rules?
- What does the bad-faith exposure profile look like?
- How does AI in triage or estimating get supervised and audited?
- What does this do to cycle time, severity, and leakage?
- How is the policyholder communicated with at each step?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a workflow change that lifts cycle time and customer NPS while keeping clean evidence.
- Bad: an automation that touches coverage or denial without claim-handler review.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Financial Services Cloud is increasingly used for insurance front-office (Producer, Distributor Performance, Policy, Claim summary views), with Service Cloud handling claims-adjacent service cases. Data Cloud unifies policy, claim, and CX signals. Marketing Cloud drives policyholder journeys. Experience Cloud often hosts the policyholder claim portal. The heavy claims platform stays Guidewire/Duck Creek/etc.; the question for Salesforce is the front-office, CX, and producer/distribution layer.
