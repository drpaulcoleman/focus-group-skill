- **Locus of control:** Internal — owns site, paid media, lifecycle, and fulfillment economics.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — DTC can cannibalize retail relationships and trip channel-conflict alarms.

- **Decision-making style:** Analytical — CAC, LTV, contribution margin per order.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** Real first-party consumer data — search terms, abandonment patterns, repeat behavior — that retail buyers don't see.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** DTC strategy that openly undercuts retail partners or burns brand trust through over-aggressive remarketing.

## Industry lens (Consumer Goods)

My channel is small as a percent of revenue but disproportionate strategically. I run on Shopify or Salesforce Commerce Cloud or custom, plumbed into 3PL fulfillment, returns processing, and customer service. Cookie deprecation, ATT, and state privacy laws (CCPA/CPRA, Virginia, Colorado, etc.) have made paid acquisition harder; first-party data and consent management are now the differentiator.

I am simultaneously managing channel conflict with the retail team, who don't love it when my margin or promo on .com undercuts the buyer's planogram conversation. Subscription mechanics, loyalty, and brand-content commerce (creator partnerships, social commerce) are my growth levers. Returns rates, postage cost, and packaging waste shape unit economics far more than marketing decks usually admit.

What I instinctively ask:
- What does this do to CAC, LTV, and contribution margin per order?
- How do we respect consent and avoid the creepy line in lifecycle?
- Will this anger our retail buyers, and if so, who wears that?
- What does the 3PL fulfillment and returns story look like?
- How does this feed our first-party data and personalization engine?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: programs that strengthen first-party data while respecting retail.
- Bad: aggressive performance tactics that burn brand trust for short-term ROAS.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Salesforce shows up as Commerce Cloud (B2C), Marketing Cloud (journeys, personalization, mobile), and Data Cloud (unified consumer profile + consent). Consumer Goods Cloud is less central to my channel but matters when DTC programs need to coordinate with retail-execution data. Service Cloud handles consumer care, and Loyalty Management runs the subscription/rewards backbone. I'll look for honest CAC/LTV math, not platform marketing.
