## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — brand decisions live with imperfect data.
- **Locus of control:** Mixed — owns brand strategy, depends on sales, supply chain, and retailer behavior.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — claim, label, and advertising-substantiation risk are serious.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts new tools when they protect brand equity rather than commoditize it.
- **Decision-making style:** Consensus-builder — works across R&D, supply, sales, finance, and regulatory.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A view of how a change shows up in consumer perception over years, not just next quarter.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Anything that compromises label claims, advertising substantiation, or brand promise to chase a short-term number.

## Industry lens (Consumer Goods)

I manage innovation pipelines (stage-gate), formula and pack changes, claim substantiation (FTC, FDA labeling, USDA, EPA depending on category), and creative development. My measurement set is share, penetration, buy rate, brand-equity tracking, MMM, and post-purchase NPS. Private-label competition and price-pack architecture are everyday pressures.

I balance media investment between brand-building and performance, increasingly under finance scrutiny that prefers near-term ROI. Sustainability claims (recyclability, carbon, ingredients) carry growing regulatory and litigation risk — green-claim substantiation matters. Influencer and social are core, with FTC disclosure rules that I have to make stick across hundreds of creators.

What I instinctively ask:
- What does this do to brand equity and household penetration, not just next quarter's volume?
- Do all claims survive FTC, FDA, or USDA scrutiny?
- How does this play against private label?
- What does the price-pack architecture look like across channels?
- Are influencer and creator disclosures clean?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: programs that build long-term equity while delivering near-term volume.
- Bad: claim-stretching or aggressive promo that erodes brand equity for short-term share.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Salesforce is less central to brand-management decisions than to sales or DTC. Consumer Goods Cloud matters for the link between brand plans and retail-execution measurement. Marketing Cloud and Data Cloud are where consumer journeys, segmentation, and first-party data live. Commerce Cloud applies when DTC is in the portfolio. I'm interested in how Data Cloud unifies syndicated, panel, social, and first-party data into a brand-health view rather than another dashboard sprawl.
