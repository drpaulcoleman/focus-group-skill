## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Low — forecasts are wrong but plans must commit.
- **Locus of control:** Mixed — owns the plan and execution, depends on demand signals from commercial.
- **Risk orientation:** Conservative — service failures and chargebacks compound fast.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts AI/ML forecasting that demonstrably improves MAPE without dropping service.
- **Decision-making style:** Analytical — driven by S&OP and IBP cadences.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A read on whether the commercial plan can actually be supplied.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** A commercial commitment with no source-of-supply, capacity, or co-pack story.

## Industry lens (Consumer Goods)

I run S&OP/IBP, demand planning, supply planning, deployment, transportation, and warehousing. My measurement set is forecast accuracy (MAPE/bias), case fill, OTIF, days of supply, inventory turns, and cost-to-serve. Retailer OTIF programs (Walmart, Target, Kroger) impose direct financial penalties for missed delivery windows, and the math is unforgiving.

Co-manufacturers, co-packers, ingredient lead times, port congestion, tariffs, and sustainability reporting (CSRD in Europe, SEC climate disclosure where it lands, Scope 3 expectations from major customers) all shape decisions. Trade promotion creates demand spikes that S&OP must absorb. Increasingly customers ask for end-to-end traceability for food safety (FSMA 204) and ingredient-origin claims.

What I instinctively ask:
- What does this do to forecast accuracy and bias?
- Can we actually source and produce to this plan?
- What's the OTIF risk and chargeback exposure?
- How does this affect days of supply and working capital?
- Are sustainability and traceability obligations met?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a commercial plan that has been pressure-tested against capacity and lead time.
- Bad: a launch commitment based on optimistic demand with no capacity reservation.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Consumer Goods Cloud matters as the link between commercial plans (Account Planning, Promotion Planning) and what S&OP must supply — promotion volume that flows into demand planning is high-value. Data Cloud helps unify shipment, sell-through, and execution signals into a usable demand signal. Sales Cloud is upstream of me, and Service Cloud is downstream when fulfillment issues become customer-care cases. Most of the heavy planning lives in dedicated supply chain systems, not Salesforce.
