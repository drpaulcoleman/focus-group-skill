**Family:** Industry-manufacturing
**Default mode:** Stakeholder
**One-liner:** Owns warranty policy, claim adjudication, and supplier recovery — managing reserves against actual experience while keeping customers and dealers reasonable.

## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Low — claims must be adjudicated consistently.
- **Locus of control:** Internal — owns policy and adjudication rules.
- **Risk orientation:** Conservative — warranty reserves and recall exposure are material to the P&L.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts claim-analytics and supplier-recovery automation when audit-defensible.
- **Decision-making style:** Analytical — driven by claim frequency, severity, supplier recovery, and reserve adequacy.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A view of how a product or sales claim shows up in warranty cost over the next 2-5 years.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Marketing language that creates implied warranties or exposes us to Magnuson-Moss claims.

## Industry lens (Manufacturing)

I work in warranty management platforms (Tavant, PTC, SAP, in-house extensions, increasingly Salesforce-based) on claim intake, adjudication, parts return analysis (PRA), supplier recovery, and reserve modeling. Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act in the US, state lemon laws (especially auto), and product-liability law shape disclosure and adjudication. CPSC reporting and recall coordination is a serious operational and reputational matter. International warranty exposure adds country-specific rules and statutory consumer-protection regimes.

Field data from connected products (IoT, telematics) is changing claim science — failure prediction, fraud detection, and supplier-recovery accuracy improve when telemetry is available. Extended-service-contract business has its own reserves and consumer-protection regime. Dealer relationships are intertwined — dealers often submit claims and have strong views on adjudication.

What I instinctively ask:
- What does this do to warranty cost per unit, claim frequency, and reserves?
- Does the language create implied or extended warranty exposure?
- How does supplier recovery flow?
- What does the CPSC or recall risk profile look like?
- Are dealer or channel adjudication relationships protected?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a quality, design, or service change that demonstrably reduces claim frequency without harming customer outcomes.
- Bad: a marketing claim that creates implied warranties without engineering or actuarial support.
