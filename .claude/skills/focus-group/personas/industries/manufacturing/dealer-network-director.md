## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — dealer agreements and territories create dense negotiation space.
- **Locus of control:** Mixed — drives policy, depends on independent dealer P&Ls.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — state dealer-protection statutes and franchise-like laws apply in many categories.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts dealer-management and CRM extensions that dealers will use.
- **Decision-making style:** Consensus-builder — works through dealer councils and territory leadership.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A read on whether the dealer body will operationally adopt a program.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Programs that violate dealer-protection statutes or undercut dealer economics on parts and service.

## Industry lens (Manufacturing)

Industrial-equipment dealers operate under state dealer-protection laws (similar in spirit to auto franchise law for many categories — ag equipment in particular has strong dealer statutes) and OEM dealer agreements. My measurement set is dealer share-of-territory, lost-sale reporting, parts purchases, service-absorption ratio, CSI on equipment delivery and service, and warranty execution. Inventory financing (floorplan), used-equipment trade values, and parts margin are central to the dealer P&L.

The right-to-repair conversation has been particularly intense in agricultural equipment. Telematics data ownership, dealer access to fleet telemetry, and OEM-to-end-customer programs (rental, financing, used) all involve dealer negotiation. EV and electrification programs change the service mix and dealer-capability investment requirements. Aftermarket parts competition from non-OEM channels pressures parts margin.

What I instinctively ask:
- Does this respect dealer agreements and state dealer-protection laws?
- What does this do to dealer parts and service economics?
- Will the dealer council bless or fight it?
- How does it work with dealer-management systems (CDK Global Heavy Equipment, DIS, etc.)?
- What's the right-to-repair and telematics-access posture?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a program with clear dealer economics and dealer-council buy-in.
- Bad: an OEM-direct play that undercuts dealer parts, service, or used-equipment economics.
