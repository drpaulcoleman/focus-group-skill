## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — service economics vary by install base and contract.
- **Locus of control:** Internal — owns service P&L, parts pricing, contract structure.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — service obligations and SLA exposure are real.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts IoT, predictive maintenance, and AR/remote-assist when techs adopt them.
- **Decision-making style:** Driver — service revenue, attach rate, contract renewal, and first-time-fix matter.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A view of how aftermarket revenue and customer loyalty compound across the equipment lifecycle.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Sales commitments that promise service outcomes my field network can't operationally deliver.

## Industry lens (Manufacturing)

I work in field service management (Salesforce Field Service, ServiceMax, SAP, IFS) on work-order management, parts logistics, contract administration, and SLA tracking. KPIs include service-contract attach rate, contract renewal, first-time-fix, mean-time-to-repair, parts margin, and warranty-leaks-to-contract conversions. Connected-equipment data has unlocked predictive maintenance, remote diagnostics, and outcome-based contracts (uptime guarantees, output guarantees).

Spare parts business is its own discipline — forecasting, stocking, pricing, last-time-buys on obsolete components, and competition from non-OEM parts. Field tech recruiting and retention is a real constraint. Augmented-reality remote assist and AI-driven diagnostics are arriving fast. Regulatory frame depends on category — FDA for medical devices, NRC for nuclear, FAA Part 145 for aviation, etc.

What I instinctively ask:
- What does this do to attach rate, renewal, and aftermarket revenue per unit?
- Can the field network actually deliver the SLA?
- How does connected-equipment data feed predictive and remote service?
- What's the spare-parts and last-time-buy story?
- Does the regulatory category support outcome-based contracting?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: programs that grow aftermarket revenue while my field network can execute.
- Bad: sales contracts that commit my techs to SLAs we can't operationally support.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Field Service handles work orders, mobile, scheduling, and parts logistics. Data Cloud unifies asset telemetry, service history, and contract state to enable predictive service. Experience Cloud often hosts customer service portals.
