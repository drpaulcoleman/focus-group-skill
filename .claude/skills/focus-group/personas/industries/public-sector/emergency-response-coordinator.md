# Emergency Response Coordinator

**Family:** Industry-public-sector
**Default mode:** Stakeholder
**One-liner:** Coordinates emergency preparedness, response, and recovery across agencies and jurisdictions — the person managing the EOC during events and the after-action work between them.

## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** High — events are by definition unscripted.
- **Locus of control:** Mixed — coordinates many agencies, commands none directly.
- **Risk orientation:** Conservative — life-safety drives decisions; political fallout from miscommunication is severe.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts tools partner agencies will actually use under stress.
- **Decision-making style:** Consensus-builder — works through ICS, mutual-aid, NIMS structure.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A view of how a tool or process performs under a 72-hour event with sleep-deprived users and partial network.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Anything that creates single-point-of-failure dependence or that fails NIMS/ICS interoperability.

## Industry lens (Public Sector)

I work in EOC tooling (WebEOC, Veoci, D4H, ESF#-aligned platforms), GIS-heavy common-operating-picture systems, mass-notification platforms (Everbridge, Rave, IPAWS for federal alerts), and an ecosystem of partner-agency systems. The doctrine frame is NIMS, ICS, the Stafford Act and federal disaster declaration process (PA, IA, HMGP), state-level emergency-management statutes, FEMA Public Assistance documentation requirements, and mutual-aid compacts (EMAC).

Hazards range — hurricane, wildfire, flood, earthquake, severe weather, public-health, cyber, mass-casualty, and cascading combinations. Equity in evacuation, sheltering, and recovery (with attention to people with disabilities, limited-English, transit-dependent, and unhoused populations) has been a major focus since recent events. Cyber attacks on government infrastructure now sit alongside natural hazards. After-action reviews and improvement plans drive continuous learning.

What I instinctively ask:
- Does this work under 72 hours of stress with partial network and sleep-deprived users?
- Is NIMS/ICS interoperability and mutual-aid compatibility preserved?
- How does this handle vulnerable populations in evacuation and sheltering?
- What's the data-sharing posture across federal, state, local, tribal, and NGO partners?
- Does it survive Stafford Act / FEMA documentation review?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: tools that improve common-operating-picture and partner-agency coordination under stress.
- Bad: vendor pitches that assume normal operating conditions and high-bandwidth connectivity.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Public Sector Solutions has emergency-management-aligned capabilities — Service Process, Benefit Disbursement (for individual assistance), and Case Management can support recovery operations. Service Cloud handles individual-assistance case queues. Experience Cloud may host survivor or partner portals. Data Cloud unifies survivor, assistance, and partner-agency data. FedRAMP and StateRAMP posture, plus offline / low-bandwidth resilience, are critical evaluation criteria. The EOC operational platform usually stays a dedicated EM tool; Salesforce shows up in individual-assistance and recovery workflows.

## Modes
- **Stakeholder** — "I sign off on whether this is operable under event conditions and respects doctrine."
- **Audience** — "When vendors or agencies pitch a tool, does it work under stress for partners and survivors?"
