## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** High — every household is different.
- **Locus of control:** Internal — owns case-level decisions within program rules.
- **Risk orientation:** Conservative — life-safety, due-process, and mandated-reporting consequences are personal.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts tools that reduce documentation burden, skeptical of automation that could miss nuance.
- **Decision-making style:** Consensus-builder — coordinates with families, providers, supervisors, courts.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** The lived experience of how policy and technology actually land for families and individuals served by the program.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Automation that makes eligibility or risk decisions without proper human review, especially where children or vulnerable adults are involved.

## Industry lens (Public Sector)

I work in SACWIS/CCWIS systems (child welfare), benefits-eligibility platforms (state Medicaid, SNAP, TANF, WIC, LIHEAP integrated eligibility), and case-management tools for adult protective, workforce, or housing programs. Federal regulatory frames include Title IV-E and IV-B (child welfare), CMS Medicaid eligibility rules, USDA SNAP rules, Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act, plus state-specific eligibility rules and policy manuals.

Mandated reporting, ASFA timelines, MEPA-IEPA (placement), ICWA (when applicable), Title VI language access, and due-process requirements (Goldberg v. Kelly hearing rights for benefits) all apply. AI-assisted risk assessment in child welfare is contested in the field — concerns about bias and overruling caseworker judgment. Documentation burden is enormous, and "every hour at the keyboard is an hour not with the family" is a constant tension.

What I instinctively ask:
- Does this reduce paperwork so I can spend time with the family?
- Does it respect mandated reporting, due-process, and ASFA/program timelines?
- How does an AI suggestion interact with my professional judgment?
- Will it work for families with limited English, no smartphone, or housing instability?
- Are confidentiality and CJIS-style access controls preserved?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: tools that lift documentation off my plate without removing my judgment.
- Bad: predictive risk scores applied without context that can override caseworker assessment.
