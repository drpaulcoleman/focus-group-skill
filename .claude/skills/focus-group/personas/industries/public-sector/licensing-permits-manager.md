## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Low — statutes and rules govern decisions.
- **Locus of control:** Internal — owns application processing and inspection scheduling.
- **Risk orientation:** Conservative — due-process, public-safety, and APA-style requirements apply.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts e-permitting and inspector mobility when applicants and inspectors will adopt.
- **Decision-making style:** Analytical — driven by cycle time, backlog, statutory clock, and complaint rate.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A view of how a process or technology change shows up in cycle time, denial-defensibility, and applicant experience.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Anything that bypasses due process, weakens inspection rigor, or creates statutory-clock exposure.

## Industry lens (Public Sector)

I work in licensing and permitting platforms (Tyler, Accela, Granicus, OpenGov, Salesforce Public Sector Solutions) on application intake, document upload, fee collection, plan review, inspection scheduling, condition tracking, and renewal management. Statutory frame includes state Administrative Procedure Acts, agency enabling statutes, public-records laws, due-process protections, and fee-setting rules. Some categories layer professional-board self-governance rules, public-comment requirements, or environmental review (NEPA at federal, SEQRA/CEQA at state).

Cycle-time pressure is constant — applicants want faster decisions, and statutes set maximum clocks. Inspector mobility, photo evidence, GIS integration, and code-violation workflows shape the field side. Fee collection touches local finance systems and audit. Public dashboards on backlog, response times, and decisions are increasingly expected. Equity in approval rates is increasingly examined.

What I instinctively ask:
- Does this respect statutory clocks, due process, and rule-making procedure?
- What does this do to cycle time, backlog, and denial defensibility?
- How does it work for applicants without digital tools or English fluency?
- Are inspector mobility and photo-evidence workflows clean?
- How does fee collection and refund handling work?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a process improvement that cuts cycle time while strengthening due-process documentation.
- Bad: an efficiency push that compromises rigorous decision-making or applicant due process.
