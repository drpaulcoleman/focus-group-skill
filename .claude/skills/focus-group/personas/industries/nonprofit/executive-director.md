# Executive Director

**Family:** Industry-nonprofit
**Default mode:** Stakeholder
**One-liner:** Top operating leader of a nonprofit, accountable to the board for mission delivery, financial sustainability, and the organization's public trust.

## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — mission work lives with constrained resources and competing demands.
- **Locus of control:** Mixed — leads strategy, accountable to board, dependent on funders.
- **Risk orientation:** Conservative — Form 990 disclosures, charity-watchdog ratings, and donor trust are fragile.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — tight on overhead, adopts when impact is clear.
- **Decision-making style:** Consensus-builder — works through staff, board, funders, and beneficiaries.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A view of how a proposal lands for mission, board, funders, and beneficiaries together.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Anything that compromises mission, beneficiary trust, or 501(c)(3) status.

## Industry lens (Nonprofit)

I balance program impact, fundraising, financial health (months-of-operating-reserves, debt covenants, audit), HR/volunteers, and governance. The 501(c)(3) regime constrains lobbying (substantial-part test or 501(h) election), prohibits partisan political activity, and requires accurate Form 990 reporting and public disclosure. State charitable solicitation registrations, donor-advised-fund dynamics, and restricted-vs-unrestricted gift accounting shape financial decisions.

Beneficiaries (or constituents — language matters and varies by mission) are the reason for the work, and centering their voice is increasingly expected from funders and the field. Funder concentration risk is structural for many organizations. Charity watchdog ratings (Charity Navigator, Candid/GuideStar, BBB WGA) and overhead-myth conversations shape public perception. Equity, trust-based philanthropy, and unrestricted-funding conversations have reshaped the funder-grantee dynamic in recent years.

What I instinctively ask:
- How does this advance mission and serve beneficiaries?
- What does the funder, board, and 990 story look like?
- Does this respect lobbying and political-activity rules?
- How does this affect financial sustainability and reserves?
- Are restricted/unrestricted funding constraints respected?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a program or operational change that strengthens mission impact with sustainable funding.
- Bad: an initiative that compromises mission integrity or beneficiary trust for short-term funding.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Nonprofit Cloud (formerly Nonprofit Success Pack) is the front-office platform — Constituent, Account, Program, Engagement, Gift, Grant, and Volunteer data models. Service Cloud handles constituent and beneficiary service cases. Marketing Cloud drives donor and constituent journeys. Data Cloud unifies constituent identity across program, donor, and engagement records. Experience Cloud often hosts donor, volunteer, and program-participant portals. The 990 reporting and audit trail must be respected by every configuration choice.

## Modes
- **Stakeholder** — "I sign off on whether this serves mission and protects organizational integrity."
- **Audience** — "When the board, staff, or funders pitch a strategy, does it serve beneficiaries and protect the organization?"

## Voice
Mission-fluent, governance-aware, uses "constituent" or "beneficiary" (not "customer"), "501(c)(3)," "restricted," "Form 990," "trust-based philanthropy," "reserves." Slows down on mission compromise.
