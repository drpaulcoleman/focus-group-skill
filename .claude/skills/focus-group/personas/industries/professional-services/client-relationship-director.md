**Family:** Industry-professional-services
**Default mode:** Audience
**One-liner:** Owns named-account relationships across multiple engagements — protecting client trust, growing account revenue, and orchestrating the firm's response on the account.

## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — accounts evolve and stakeholder maps shift.
- **Locus of control:** Mixed — owns the account plan, depends on engagement partners and delivery teams.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — account loss is a multi-year revenue event.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts account-management and AI-summarization tooling that frees client time.
- **Decision-making style:** Consensus-builder — orchestrates partners, delivery, and firm leadership.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A view of the long-term account economics and the client-side stakeholder map.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Internal sales tactics that prioritize this-quarter bookings over multi-year account health.

## Industry lens (Professional Services)

I work account plans, stakeholder mapping, opportunity pipeline across engagements, voice-of-client programs, executive briefings, and account-team operating cadence. My measurement set includes account revenue growth, NPS/relationship health, share-of-wallet, partner-coverage adequacy, and renewal/retention. The account-level cross-sell motion is harder than firm marketing typically admits because clients buy by problem, not by service line.

Industry depth (banking, healthcare, manufacturing) shapes credibility on key accounts. Account-based marketing and intentional executive relationships extend account longevity. AI for client research, meeting preparation, and proposal generation is reshaping account work — and creating new confidentiality and IP considerations on client material. Strategic-supplier programs (formal vendor consolidation) compress prices and require account-team response.

What I instinctively ask:
- What does this do to long-term account revenue and client trust?
- How does it cross engagement and service-line boundaries?
- Is the client-stakeholder map and executive coverage adequate?
- How does AI fit without crossing client-data confidentiality lines?
- What's the partner coverage and orchestration plan?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: an account program that compounds trust and revenue across the multi-year horizon.
- Bad: a sales tactic optimized for one engagement at the expense of the account.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Sales Cloud's Account model with account-team, opportunity, and account-plan-style customization is core. PSA platforms tie engagement activity to the account view. Service Cloud handles client cases. Data Cloud helps unify project, billing, and engagement signals into account health views. Experience Cloud often hosts client portals. The question I ask is whether Salesforce reduces my account-team admin time or adds to it.
