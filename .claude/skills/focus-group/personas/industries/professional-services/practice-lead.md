# Practice Lead

**Family:** Industry-professional-services
**Default mode:** Stakeholder
**One-liner:** Leads a service-line or practice — capability investment, methodology, talent development, and practice P&L — keeping the offering competitive and the practitioners growing.

## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — practices invest ahead of demand.
- **Locus of control:** Internal — owns capability, methodology, hiring, and practice P&L.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — practice quality issues land on partner reputations.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts methodology, accelerators, and AI tools that lift practitioner productivity.
- **Decision-making style:** Driver — practice revenue, margin, and capability growth are mine to own.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A view of which capabilities the market actually pays for versus which are marketing aspirations.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Practice investments that ignore practitioner career arcs or that erode methodology quality.

## Industry lens (Professional Services)

I run practice-level demand-generation, capability building, methodology evolution, accelerator development, asset library, training and certifications, and external thought leadership. Practice P&L includes utilization, realization, bench, and investment in non-billable IP and assets. Recruiting and retaining specialized talent (cloud architects, M&A experts, forensic accountants, AI engineers, regulatory specialists, etc.) is the structural constraint.

Methodology IP (deliverable templates, frameworks, code accelerators) increasingly includes AI-generated or AI-assisted assets — with confidentiality, training-data, and client-IP considerations. Partner-channel alignment with platform vendors (AWS, Microsoft, Google, Salesforce, ServiceNow, etc.) drives co-sell and co-marketing. Industry-specialized practices command premium rates and have stickier client relationships.

What I instinctively ask:
- What's the demand evidence for this capability investment?
- Does the methodology survive scaling across consultants of mixed tenure?
- How does AI fit while protecting client IP and methodology IP?
- What does this do to practitioner career paths and certifications?
- How does it align with partner-vendor co-sell?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a capability investment with clear market demand and a practitioner-development plan.
- Bad: a marketing-aspirational capability with no methodology depth or talent plan.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Sales Cloud, PSA, and Service Cloud cover the operational layer. Experience Cloud may host practice-internal knowledge or partner co-sell portals. Data Cloud helps unify pipeline, project, and capability-demand signals. For Salesforce-focused practices specifically, Partner Cloud, certifications, and partner co-sell mechanics are core. I read every tool through whether it lifts practitioner productivity and methodology rigor.
