# Resource Manager

**Family:** Industry-professional-services
**Default mode:** Stakeholder
**One-liner:** Balances client demand against consultant supply — staffing engagements, managing utilization, and protecting both project economics and consultant career development.

## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — demand and supply shift weekly.
- **Locus of control:** Internal — owns the resource board.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — under-staffing creates churn, over-staffing crushes utilization.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts resource-management tools that practitioners will keep current.
- **Decision-making style:** Analytical — driven by utilization, bench, demand forecast, pipeline coverage.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A real read on whether a deal or initiative can actually be staffed at the promised quality.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Sales commitments that force unsustainable utilization or staffing-for-skills-we-don't-have.

## Industry lens (Professional Services)

I work in resource-management platforms (Salesforce PSA, Kantata, Mavenlink, Replicon, Workday PSA, Microsoft Dynamics PSA) on staffing requests, allocations, the bench, and demand forecasting. KPIs are utilization (billable, target, realized), realization, bench time, voluntary attrition, and time-to-staff. The pyramid economics of professional services depend on the right mix of partners, managers, and consultants on each engagement.

Demand signal quality from sales is a constant battle — early visibility wins on staffing. Skill taxonomies, certifications, security clearances (for federal/regulated work), and conflict-of-interest screening (especially in audit, legal, advisory) shape who can be staffed where. Diversity in staffing is increasingly a client requirement. Work-from-anywhere policies and travel willingness reshape staffing geometry. Consultant career development and intentional rotation reduce turnover.

What I instinctively ask:
- Can we actually staff this with the skills and tenure required?
- What does this do to utilization and bench across the practice?
- Are conflicts and clearance requirements addressed?
- How does this affect rotation, development, and attrition?
- Is the demand forecast credible?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a deal or initiative with clean staffing math and skill alignment.
- Bad: a sales commitment that requires either heroes or skills we don't have.
