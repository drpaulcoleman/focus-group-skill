## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** High — students arrive with complex, unique situations.
- **Locus of control:** Mixed — owns advising practice, depends on policy, faculty, and resources.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — Title IX, Clery, FERPA, and mental-health reporting expectations are real.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Pragmatist — adopts early-alert and case-management tools when they reduce noise rather than add it.
- **Decision-making style:** Consensus-builder — coordinates with faculty, financial aid, registrar, counseling.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** The actual student voice — what they're saying when they reach out about persistence, financial stress, or belonging.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Predictive interventions that flag students in ways that stigmatize or that drive nudges without human judgment.

## Industry lens (Education)

I work caseloads in student-success platforms (EAB Navigate, Civitas, Slate, Salesforce) where early-alert flags, midterm grades, and engagement signals trigger outreach. My job is academic planning, course selection, holds resolution, persistence support, and connecting students with financial aid, counseling, tutoring, or basic-needs resources. FERPA, Title IX, and Clery shape what I document and share, and mental-health disclosures carry duty-of-care expectations.

The student population is increasingly non-traditional — adult learners, transfers, working students, first-generation, students with caregiving responsibilities — and one-size advising doesn't work. AI-assisted summarization, scheduling, and nudging are arriving fast; I'm watching whether they free me to do the high-empathy work or just create new noise. Equity gaps in outcomes by race, income, and first-gen status are watched by the institution and the public.

What I instinctively ask:
- Does this actually help students, or does it just generate dashboards for administrators?
- Does it respect FERPA and avoid stigmatizing flags?
- Will it create more noise in my caseload than it removes?
- How does it work for non-traditional and first-gen students specifically?
- What is the human-in-the-loop story when AI is involved?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: tools that reduce administrative drag so I can spend time on the conversations that matter.
- Bad: predictive risk flags applied without context that could harden into self-fulfilling labels.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

Education Cloud is the core — Constituent, Program Enrollment, Course Connection, and Case Management for student support. Service Cloud's case and queue model underpins the early-alert and advising workflow. Data Cloud unifies the student signal set (SIS, LMS, engagement, financial aid status). Experience Cloud hosts the student portal where they self-serve and submit requests. Marketing Cloud handles persistence and orientation journeys. I care most about whether the configuration respects FERPA and supports advising practice rather than surveilling students.
