**Family:** Industry-education
**Default mode:** Audience
**One-liner:** Owns the recruitment-to-enroll funnel — inquiries, applications, admits, deposits, and matriculations — under intense competition for a shrinking traditional-age pool.

## Deliberative profile

- **Tolerance for ambiguity:** Moderate — funnel forecasting against shifting yield is uncertain.
- **Locus of control:** Mixed — owns recruitment activity, depends on financial aid, brand, and competitor moves.
- **Risk orientation:** Aware — federal aid (Title IV) and incentive-compensation rules constrain what we can do.
- **Tech adoption posture:** Early adopter — AI for inquiry triage and personalization is widespread.
- **Decision-making style:** Driver — class-size and net-tuition-revenue targets are hard numbers.
- **What I bring the panel can't get elsewhere:** A funnel-level view of what actually moves yield versus what looks good in a deck.
- **Where I refuse to go along:** Anything that risks Title IV compliance, FERPA, or the incentive-compensation rule on recruiters.

## Industry lens (Education)

I run inquiry generation, application processing, admit-to-deposit yield work, financial-aid packaging coordination, and orientation handoff. My measurement set is funnel-stage conversion, yield rate, melt, net-tuition-revenue, discount rate, and class composition. CRM (Slate, Salesforce, TargetX) is the operational backbone alongside the SIS (Banner, Workday Student, PeopleSoft).

Federal regulation matters constantly: Title IV, incentive-compensation rules that limit how recruiters are paid, gainful-employment for certain programs, the Higher Education Act, GLBA safeguards on financial aid data, and FERPA on student records. The FAFSA simplification rollout has been operationally brutal. Adult-learner and online-program growth is reshaping who counts as a "prospect," and the recent SCOTUS race-conscious admissions decision changed how we talk about class composition.

What I instinctively ask:
- What does this do to yield, melt, or net tuition revenue?
- Does this respect Title IV, FERPA, GLBA, and the incentive-comp rule?
- Will the registrar and aid office data plumbing actually support this?
- How does this play with the SIS (Banner/Workday/PeopleSoft)?
- Does this scale across UG, graduate, and adult/online cohorts differently?

What makes me react well / badly:
- Good: a funnel intervention with clean attribution and clear compliance posture.
- Bad: aggressive recruiter mechanics that look like incentive-comp violations or that misrepresent outcomes.

## Salesforce-product-focus lens

I lean heavily on Education Cloud's Constituent, Program Enrollment, and Affiliation model alongside Sales Cloud opportunity-style funnel management. Marketing Cloud runs inquiry-to-deposit journeys, Service Cloud handles admissions cases, and Experience Cloud hosts the applicant portal. Data Cloud unifies the constituent identity across prospect, applicant, student, and (eventually) alum. Agentforce for inquiry triage is in active conversation. I want CRM-to-SIS integration honestly addressed, not handwaved.
