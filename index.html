What this is

Six skill files for Claude Code or Cursor. You drop them in a folder; the slash commands appear in autocomplete.

What it isn't

Not a CRM plugin, not a Salesforce add-on, not a paid service. Nothing to provision, no account to create, nothing to wire into your org.

What changes for you

What changes for you — three things you walk in with

🎯

Their tough questions, raised in writing first.

The Economic Buyer asking "and the ROI in our numbers?" · the InfoSec officer asking "who actually sees the data?" · the Board Treasurer asking "what does year three cost?" · the Champion's manager asking "is this what we approved?" — in your inbox before the meeting, with time to answer them well.

📚

You sound prepared because you are.

When a customer asks "what's the actual difference between X and Y?", you have it — cited to the real doc. The skills do the product reading you don't have time for, so every claim in your draft is backed by a source you can point to.

🛡️

Customer trust, built into the workflow.

The skills scrub identifiable customer data before any AI sees it — safe by default, even when you forget to think about it. You move faster and protect the relationships and the legal posture that make the deals worth winning.

Meet the panel

Meet the panel — six skills, one workflow

Each skill earns its keep on its own. Together they take you from cold prospect research to a draft your customer's InfoSec officer can't pick apart.

👥

/focus-group

The headliner.

Pressure-test your draft against the five people who'll hear it first. The skill picks a panel sized to your customer — Solution Engineer, Champion, Economic Buyer, InfoSec, Industry Specialist — each reacts from their own seat, then a synthesis pass hands you a ranked action list with citations.

Reach for it when you have a draft and a call coming up.
Try: /focus-group review my discovery questions for Acme Credit Union
📖

/download

The librarian.

Stop guessing what the docs actually say. The skill pulls in the real Salesforce Help, Trailhead, Architect decision guides, and developer.salesforce.com pages on your topic, stores them locally, and lets the panel cite them in its review.

Reach for it when you don't trust the AI to remember a product detail.
Try: /download agentforce data cloud for healthcare patient services
⚖️

/cross-ai-review

Two or three sets of eyes, not one.

Two or three AI models read your draft side-by-side. What they all flag is probably real; what only one flags is where the deal-killers usually hide. You get an accuracy score before you send.

Reach for it when the draft is going to a customer and one model's word isn't enough.
Try: /cross-ai-review --prompt-file ./proposal-section-3.md --require-citations
🔐

/anonymize

The guardrail.

Talk to AI about your real customers without the legal blast radius. Names, emails, ARR figures, and account IDs become typed placeholders before any model sees them — and get restored in the final report. The token map stays on your laptop.

Reach for it when you'd be uncomfortable seeing the raw text in a public AI's logs.
Auto-runs inside the other skills. Or standalone: /anonymize <paste>
💬

/slackbot

The team radar.

Pull the freshest team intel before a customer call. The skill surfaces recent mentions of the account across the channels you're already in — deal rooms, customer Slack Connect, ops threads — and routes everything through /anonymize on the way out. Never DMs, never channels you aren't a member of.

Reach for it when you're prepping a status call and want the latest internal context.
Try: /slackbot Acme Credit Union
✨

/how-can-this-be-improved

The sharpening pass.

Catches the hedges, the buried bottom line, the audience the panel didn't cover — before they reach a customer. Point it at the report your /focus-group run just produced, or any draft, and you get a sharper version back.

Reach for it when the panel just finished and you want a fresh-eye pass before you send anything on.
Try: /how-can-this-be-improved .scratch/focus-group/2026-05-21-acme-qbr.md

Examples

Prompt examples

Copy any of these into Claude Code or Cursor. They're organized from quick gut checks to multi-skill chains that prep a full onsite. Read each one as: "the moment I'd reach for this", then "what I get back."

The default flow: four quick questions the first time, then mostly cached

When you invoke /focus-group cold for the first time in a workspace, the skill walks you through four short questions — each a one-second tap on a Cursor / Claude Code chat option. The first question is the one that shapes everything that follows: what's your role? The final report gets framed in your job's language — talk tracks for an Account Executive, demo-flow gotchas for a Solution Engineer, ROI math for a Business Value Consultant, integration sequencing for an Architect.

  1. Your role. Account Executive · Solution Engineer · Industry Specialist · Enterprise / Technical Architect · Business Value Consultant · Lead Engagement · Customer Success Manager · Partner Account Manager · Other. Asked once and remembered (with your consent) — see the bottom mockup.
  2. The subject. A file in this repo · pasted block of text · URL to fetch first · described scenario · other. Asked every time — different content each run.
  3. Product / industry context. Default salesforce-crm-agentforce; you can switch packs or layer an industry on top. Remembers your last pick; skipped on subsequent runs unless you say otherwise.
  4. The customer. Profile by name · profile by culture & size · skip. Saved per workspace; refreshed only after 14 days or when you ask.

Here's what each of those prompts actually looks like in the chat pane — first the role question, then (after subject / product / customer are settled) the panel-recommendation prompt:

After Steps 2–4 (subject, product/industry, customer) are settled, the skill assembles a 5-persona panel and asks for your sign-off. Same chat-text format — type a number to swap or drop someone, or just say "OK":

Set it once. After that, usually one question.

The pipeline has 12 steps — preflight, research, persona dispatch, synthesis, citations, save — and most run silently. Only a few ask you anything. First run: typically four prompts (role, product pack, customer profile, panel approval). Every run after that, the panel is already shaped around your role, your usual product, and the customer on file — and the skill just asks what do you want me to review?

The examples below assume that first-run setup is done.

Quick wins — one skill, one purpose

When you want a fast gut check
/focus-group help me understand a SE and a CIO Economic Buyer's perspective on Agentforce for contact-center deflection

Runs a two-persona panel on a generic prompt — no customer named, no downloads. Five-second framing pass; useful while you're getting your own argument straight.

When the call is tomorrow and you haven't pre-researched
/focus-group next-call prep on Service Cloud Voice for a mid-market retail customer — attendees are an AE, an SE, the customer's VP Customer Care (Champion), and a CIO Economic Buyer

The skill asks once whether to profile by name or by culture/size. Pick "by culture & size" for the wizard. Five-minute pass.

When you don't trust the AI to remember a product detail
/download agentforce data cloud grounding for healthcare patient services

Because the topic mentions Salesforce, the skill automatically searches help.salesforce.com, Trailhead, Architect, and developer.salesforce.com alongside the general web. Writes a ranked list of sources you can pick from, and offers to grab the top few in full.

Errors-and-omissions audit before a claim goes out
/cross-ai-review --prompt "Salesforce Health Cloud has native FHIR R4 support that maps Patient and Encounter objects without custom code. Is this accurate as of today?"

You find out before your customer does whether the claim holds up — and what you didn't say that the customer would expect to hear. Two or three AI models compare notes; the report flags errors (claims that don't hold up) and omissions (context they'd expect to see). If you only have Claude installed, it runs Opus + Sonnet in parallel. Comes with an accuracy score so you know how much to trust it.

Want a specific panel?

Just name it. A quick-pick panel by name (with the Buyer panel), an industry pack as a switch (--industry healthcare-life-sciences), or natural-language guidance (--personas "must include HIPAA Privacy Officer; keep it to 4"). The skill biases its recommendation, then still asks you to confirm before the panel runs.

Custom panel, industry-loaded, in one line
/focus-group review my Health Cloud + Agentforce demo flow --industry healthcare-life-sciences --personas "must include HIPAA Privacy Officer, Director of Patient Experience; skip the investor family"

Loads the healthcare-life-sciences pack (CMO, Patient Experience Director, Clinical Trials Ops, Payer Network Director, HIPAA Privacy Officer) and biases the recommendation toward the seats you named. Quick-pick panels available out of the box: Buyer, Tech, Discovery, Demo Readiness, Architecture Review, Industry Deep-Dive, Everyone. Browse all 19 industry packs →

Non-Salesforce work — the --generic escape hatch
/focus-group --generic --personas "Solution Architect, AI/Agent Architect, Security Reviewer, Beta Tester, Customer Success Manager" pressure-test my plan to build a contract-renewal-prediction model for any mid-market B2B SaaS

--generic bypasses Salesforce product + industry grounding entirely. Personas use only their generic lens — no Salesforce framing, no Salesforce-customer questions. Useful when you're prepping non-Salesforce work (a side project, a tool evaluation, an internal proposal) and you still want the panel's deliberative spread without the framing assuming Salesforce CRM. The customer-grounding gate is skipped too, so you go straight to panel composition.

Sharper plays — two skills, a named customer

Discovery prep for a named prospect
/focus-group I have discovery on Wednesday with Acme Credit Union — they're a prospect; here are my draft discovery questions: ...

Stops at Step 3, asks "profile by name" → you say yes, "prospect or current customer?" → you say prospect. Offers /download against Acme's public sources (About, IR, LinkedIn company page, Crunchbase) and proposes a panel of 5 sized to the org: Solution Engineer, Industry Specialist (Financial Services), Champion (VP Member Experience), CIO Economic Buyer, Business Value Consultant.

Chain: /focus-group → /download (public sources) → /anonymize (every persona prompt) → /cross-ai-review (cross-model check) → restored final report with citations.

Quarterly Business Review review for an existing customer (Slack-grounded)
/focus-group review this QBR draft for Pacific Grants Alliance @QBR-draft.pdf

Step 3 → "by name" → "current customer". The skill detects Slack Model Context Protocol and offers /slackbot Pacific Grants Alliance as one of the data-source boxes (alongside sf account / opps / cases / activity). On approval, queries the channels you're in, summarizes the open threads and recent sentiment, anonymizes the whole thing, presents the summary for your final OK — then hands it onward to the panel.

Chain: /focus-group → /slackbot → Salesforce account data → /anonymize → panel runs → /cross-ai-review cross-check → /anonymize restore → final report.

Strict-citation review for a written proposal
/focus-group --require-citations review proposal-section-3.md for accuracy and FUD bait

Any factual claim without a /download-backed source goes into a "Needs verification" section. Accuracy score caps at 70 until the gaps close. The skill offers to run /download against a seed list from the active product/industry pack to fill them.

The big plays — multi-skill chains, real account context

"How do I approach this existing customer with Agentforce?"
/focus-group How should I approach the New South Wales Department of Education about Agentforce for their student-services contact centre? They're already on Service Cloud + Education Cloud, four years in. Champion is the Director of Student Wellbeing. Here's my current talk track: ...

This is the bread-and-butter play. The skill walks through:

  1. Customer grounding — picks up NSW Department of Education by name; you confirm current customer; consent menu for sf data; offers /slackbot for recent context. Everything routes through /anonymize.
  2. Topic grounding — /download seed list for Agentforce + Education Cloud (Agentforce overview, Education Cloud articles, a Trailhead module on Atlas reasoning).
  3. Panel composition — recommends 5 personas sized to the customer: Solution Engineer (Service Cloud + Education Cloud), Industry Specialist (Education / Public Sector), Privacy Officer (Australian Privacy Principles & state data law), Champion (Director of Student Wellbeing), Business Value Consultant. Swap as you like.
  4. Run + pull together — spread across your available AI command-line tools in parallel; a synthesis pass aggregates the persona feedback, a second model cross-checks it, and the final write-up arrives with an accuracy score.
  5. Final report — real names and account context restored; cites the 4–6 sources /download harvested; flags persona divergence and under-cited claims.

Typical run: 6–10 minutes elapsed. Saved to .scratch/focus-group/<date>-nsw-education-agentforce.md for next time.

Cross-cloud whitespace — sequence the right cloud first
/focus-group This customer is heavy on Sales Cloud but has no Service Cloud, no Marketing Cloud, and no Data Cloud yet — and they need at least two of those. Help me sequence the conversation so I land the right one first without losing the others. Customer: BankCo Regional. Champion: VP Retail Banking.

Layers the financial-services industry pack on top of the default salesforce-crm-agentforce product pack. The skill notices the cross-cloud framing and adds: Enterprise Architect (sequencing), Customer Success Manager (adoption risk), Cost & Budget Owner (total cost of ownership across clouds) on top of the standard Solution Engineer / Champion / Economic Buyer. The write-up is explicit about which cloud to lead with and why, with dissenting voices preserved.

"Art of the Possible" onsite visit plan
/focus-group --everyone plan an Art-of-the-Possible onsite for Mountain Region Community Foundation: 4-hour session, audience is the Executive Director + CIO + 3 program directors + Director of Development. Demo flow touches Agentforce, Data Cloud, and Nonprofit Cloud with grants-and-programs integration as the through-line. Here are last month's customer notes and the sf account snapshot: ...

--everyone broadens the panel for an onsite of this scale. Expect 10–14 personas: Solution Engineer × 2 (Service Cloud and Data Cloud), Enterprise Architect, Industry Specialist (Healthcare), HIPAA Privacy Officer, CMIO equivalent, Patient Experience Director, Business Value Consultant, Implementation Partner (delivery reality), AppExchange ISV partner (data-grounding partners), Customer Success Manager. The write-up warns it's broader-but-shallower; produces a sequenced agenda with named demo slots, talk-track suggestions per persona, and the risks to pre-empt.

Chain: full stack — /focus-group → /slackbot (last month) → Salesforce account pull (active opportunities + open cases, via Model Context Protocol) → /anonymize → /download (Agentforce + Data Cloud + Nonprofit Cloud overviews + 1–2 customer references) → panel × 12 → first pass, cross-check, and final write-up → final restored report. 20–40 minutes elapsed for an onsite that matters.

Vibe-coding a demo spec
/focus-group help me sketch a demo build spec for a Nonprofit Cloud + Agentforce vibe-code that shows a program-intake assistant routing a new beneficiary request from a phone summary to the right case worker. Customer is Northwest Watershed Conservancy; demo is Friday.

Recommends a vibe-coding quick-pick: Solution Engineer, Technical Architect, AI/Agent Architect, Beta Tester, Customer Success Manager — plus the Champion to keep the demo grounded in their actual workflow. The first pass drafts the spec itself (objects, prompt template shapes, governor-limit concerns, HITL placement, what to fake vs build). The cross-model check catches gaps before you start building.

Standalone fact-check before a customer email
/anonymize "Sarah Patel at Mountain Region Community Foundation asked about Agentforce limits for their 8,400-volunteer engagement program with $620K in annual subscription spend."

/cross-ai-review --prompt-file ./email-draft.md --require-citations

Two-step: sanitize the draft so the customer's name and ARR don't leak to the cross-AI side-by-side check. Then run the audit on the anonymized text. The accuracy score and citations land in ~/.claude/cross-ai-runs/<run-id>/report.md; restore with /anonymize restore before pasting back.

Industry deep-dive briefing
/focus-group --industry public-sector --product service-cloud Brief me on the top 3 objections a State CIO would raise to a Service Cloud + Agentforce constituent-services pitch. Use the State of Oregon as a thinking proxy.

Loads the public-sector industry pack — Constituent Services Director, Licensing & Permits Manager, Emergency Response Coordinator, Case Worker, IT Modernization Director — plus standard Salesforce-sales personas. The "thinking proxy" framing keeps the prompt generic so no real customer data is involved.

"Run by every AI you have" final check
/cross-ai-review --prompt-file ./proposal-final.md --require-citations --attach customer-account-snapshot.md

If you have Claude + Codex + Gemini installed, fans the proposal to all three in parallel. If only Claude, runs Opus + Sonnet. The Accuracy score and Citations block tell you whether you've grounded enough to send. The attached snapshot gives every model the same customer context (already anonymized).

Customization

Make it your own — sound like your customers

The skills ship with generic Salesforce coverage — solid, but generic. Your deals are narrower: a specific role, a specific vertical, the customers you actually sit across from. Sharpening the skills to your work is itself a Claude prompt away.

Make a persona push back the way your real customers do
Sharpen the HIPAA Privacy Officer persona to push back the way the privacy officers I sell to actually do — Epic / Cerner integration risk, Hyperforce data residency for PHI, the EHR-to-Service-Cloud round-trip latency conversations. Show me the diff before you save.

Claude reads the persona file, proposes targeted edits with diff preview, and lets you accept or revise. You walk away with a persona that argues like the privacy officers you see — not a stock placeholder.

Same pattern works for anything else you'd want to tune: add a K-12 variant of the education pack, build a "Renewal" quick-pick panel for your team's motion, layer in APAC procurement context, or add a coaching-mode that swaps the toughest critics for mentor versions. Ask Claude what you want; review the diff; ship it.

For a second pass on any of these edits, run /how-can-this-be-improved <path-to-skill> — it audits the skill for drift, edge cases, and consistency with the rest of the suite. Local-only; never calls external models on its own.
